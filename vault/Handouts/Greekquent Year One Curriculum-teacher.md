# Greekquent Year One: Phonetics-First Curriculum
Late October – mid May · 1 lesson/week · 60 minutes · 26 lessons

Anchor verbs: έχω · θέλω · μπορώ · βλέπω, introduced in that order and recycled in every later lesson.

Student pages for every lesson are in the companion file, Greekquent_Year_One_Curriculum-student.md.

---

## 1. How the year is built

| Phase | Lessons | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| A. Alphabet & sound | 1–6 | All 24 letters at 4 per week, plus the main digraphs. Use the four anchor verbs in the singular, with yes/no answers and negation. |
| B. Building on the four | 7–16 | Add είμαι, plurals, κάνω, πάω, numbers, food, time, and a mid-year check. |
| C. Real-world Greek | 17–26 | Future with θα, directions, likes, past tense (the four verbs first), Easter, and a capstone. |

### Design rules (apply to every lesson)

1. Students only read and write letters they've already learned. Speaking runs ahead of reading. Students can say "Δεν μπορώ" in Lesson 3 before they can read the δ in Lesson 5. In the transcripts, oral-only material is marked (oral).
2. Sight chunks. A few social words are allowed as whole-word "pictures" before every letter is known: Γεια σου/σας, ευχαριστώ, Καλημέρα. They're marked with *.
3. Teach one new anchor verb per week, and keep the old ones in play. Every warm-up and every scenario uses at least two anchor verbs.
4. In weeks 1–3, use only neuter nouns. That keeps ένα and το from changing, so gender doesn't get in the way of the verb. Masculine and feminine come in as chunks in Lesson 4.
5. Use 8–10 new words per lesson, and only one new grammar idea.
6. Expanding review. Each warm-up pulls items from 1, 2, 4, and 8 lessons back (see Section 5).
7. Daily homework of about 10 minutes. With a 7-day gap between lessons, short daily practice is what keeps once-a-week classes from fading.

### Letter sequence (chosen so the anchor verbs become readable fast)

Classic α-β-γ order doesn't let students read έχω until the end of week 6. This order puts each verb's letters in early:

| Lesson | Letters | Digraphs | Unlocks reading of |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ε ε · Χ χ · Ω ω · Λ λ | — | έχω, λέω |
| 2 | Θ θ · Ι ι · Σ σ ς · Α α | ει | θέλω, θέλεις, έχεις, αλλά |
| 3 | Μ μ · Π π · Ο ο · Ρ ρ | μπ | μπορώ, ώρα, μέρα, πολλά |
| 4 | Β β · Ν ν · Τ τ · Κ κ | αι | βλέπω, βιβλίο, και, να, τι, καλά, παρακαλώ |
| 5 | Η η · Υ υ · Δ δ · Γ γ | ου, οι | δεν, εδώ, μου/σου, πού, γιατί |
| 6 | Φ φ · Ζ ζ · Ξ ξ · Ψ ψ | αυ/ευ, ντ, γκ/γγ, τσ, τζ | ευχαριστώ, αυτό, ξέρω, ψωμί, everything |

24 letters ÷ 4 per week = 6 weeks.

### Standard 60-minute arc

| Time | Block | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Warm-up and spaced review | Retrieval of old material, before any new input |
| 0:10–0:25 | New sounds and letters (Phase A), or new language input (B/C) | Students listen, then repeat, then read |
| 0:25–0:42 | Anchor-verb work | Input first, then controlled practice, then free practice |
| 0:42–0:54 | Scenario role-play | Students use the language in a real situation, in pairs |
| 0:54–1:00 | Wrap-up, exit ticket, homework | Each student produces one sentence alone |

Aim for students talking 60%+ of the practice blocks. Raise the share of your own talk that's in Greek over the year, from about 20% in October to about 70% by April.

## 3. Full lesson transcripts: Phase A (Lessons 1–6)

Notation: T = teacher, S = student(s), Ss = all students together (choral). Pronunciation respellings are for American speakers: kh = the throaty sound in "Bach", th = "think", dh = "this", CAPS = stressed syllable.

### Lesson 1: Ε · Χ · Ω · Λ | έχω

**At a glance**
- Letters: Ε ε /e/ · Χ χ /x/ · Ω ω /o/ · Λ λ /l/
- Readable words: έχω (EH-kho), λέω (LEH-o)
- Anchor verb: έχω (first person only); έχεις; heard only as a question
- New nouns (oral, all neuter): βιβλίο, στυλό, κινητό, κλειδί, νερό, τετράδιο
- Chunks (oral): Γεια σας / Γεια σου, Πώς σε λένε; Με λένε…, Ναι / Όχι, ένα, Τι έχεις;
- Materials: a bag holding the 6 real objects, letter cards, a whiteboard

**0:00–0:10 Welcome (oral)**
T: (waves to the group) Γεια σας!
Ss: Γεια σας!
T: (turns to one student, smiles) Γεια σου.
T (English): σας is for a group or for being polite. σου is for one friend.
T: (hand on chest) Με λένε [name]. (gestures to student) Πώς σε λένε;
S1: Με λένε ___.
T: Γεια σου, ___!
(Go around the room. Then pairs repeat the exchange with each other.)
T: (nods) Ναι. (shakes head) Όχι. Ναι… όχι… (Ss copy the gestures and words.)

**0:10–0:25 Letters and sounds**
T writes ε.
T: This is έψιλον. Its sound is "e" as in "pet", short and clean. Never "ee."
T: ε… ε… ε. → Ss: ε, ε, ε.

T writes ω.
T: ωμέγα. It's "o" as in "or" without the r. Keep it pure, with no slide into "oh-oo." Greek vowels never glide.
Ss: ω, ω, ω. (T models the American "go-w" as the wrong version. Ss laugh, then do it right.)

T writes λ.
T: λάμδα. A light "l", tongue tip just behind the top teeth. Not the heavy "l" in "full."
Ss: λε, λω. (Pair λ only with the vowels students already know.)

T writes χ.
T: χι. It's not English "x" and not the "ch" in "church." Say "k" but don't close your throat all the way, and let air scrape through, like "Bach" or "loch."
Ss: χ… χ… χω.
(Preview: next week χ gets softer before ε/ι.)

T writes the accent mark ´ above a vowel.
T: This is the τόνος. It marks the stressed syllable. Every Greek word with two or more syllables has one.
Clap test: Έ-χω (clap on Έ). Λέ-ω (clap on Λέ).

Syllable ladder (read by everyone, then pairs, then individuals):
`λε – λω – χε – χω – ελ – εχ – ωλ`
`έχω · λέω`

Capitals check: Ε Χ Ω Λ. Capitals are for names and sentence starts. We read mostly lowercase.

**0:25–0:42 Anchor verb: έχω**
Step 1: listen only (2 min). T pulls each object out of the bag.
T: Έχω ένα βιβλίο. (holds up book) Έχω ένα στυλό. Έχω ένα κινητό. Έχω ένα κλειδί. Έχω νερό. Έχω ένα τετράδιο.
Step 2: point. T: Κλειδί; Ss point. Βιβλίο; Ss point. (Do this fast and mix the order.)
Step 3: choral repeat. T: Έχω ένα βιβλίο. → Ss: Έχω ένα βιβλίο. (Do all six.)
Step 4: hand out the objects. T: (to S1, who holds the key) Έχεις ένα κλειδί;
S1: Ναι, έχω.
T: (to S2, empty-handed) Έχεις ένα κινητό;
S2: Όχι.
T (English, once only): έχω = I have. έχεις = you have. A question is the same words with your voice rising.
Step 5: chain. S1: Έχω ένα στυλό. (passes it) → S2: Έχω ένα στυλό. … Keep it moving fast.

**0:42–0:54 Scenario: "Τι έχεις;" bag guessing game**
Each student hides 3 objects or picture cards in a bag. Partners guess.
S1: Έχεις ένα κινητό;
S2: Ναι, έχω! / Όχι.
1 point for each right guess. T walks around and models Τι έχεις; ("What do you have?") so students can ask openly at the end.

**0:54–1:00 Wrap-up**
Dictation: T says λε, χω, έχω, λέω. Ss write them, then check with a partner.
Exit ticket: each student says one true sentence. Έχω ένα ___.

**Homework (10 min/day)**
1. Write ε χ ω λ five times each, and read the syllable ladder aloud.
2. Flashcards with audio for the 6 nouns.
3. Every day, touch 3 objects at home and say "Έχω ένα ___."

**Teacher watch-list:** χ said as "k"; ω with an "oh-oo" glide; stress on the wrong syllable.

### Lesson 2: Θ · Ι · Σ/ς · Α | θέλω

**At a glance**
- Letters: Θ θ /θ/ · Ι ι /i/ · Σ σ ς /s/ · Α α /a/
- Digraph: ει = /i/ ("ee")
- Readable: θέλω, θέλεις, θέλει, έχεις, έχει, λες, λέει, αλλά, άλλα, Θεσσαλία
- Anchor verb: θέλω; plus έχω extended to έχεις / έχει
- New nouns (oral, neuter, things to offer): τσάι, γάλα, κρασί, ψωμί, γλυκό (+ νερό, review)
- Chunks: λίγο, *ευχαριστώ, Όχι, *ευχαριστώ, ο Νίκος / η Μαρία (oral)

**0:00–0:10 Spaced review (Lesson 1)**
T: (holds up objects) Τι έχω;
Ss: Έχεις ένα βιβλίο! (T models έχεις if needed. It's the first time students produce it.)
Quick pair round: Έχεις ένα κλειδί; – Ναι, έχω. / Όχι.
Letter flash: show ε χ ω λ. Ss give each sound in under 2 seconds.

**0:10–0:25 Letters and sounds**
θ θήτα: "th" as in "think", never as in "this." Tongue between the teeth, air only. Ss: θε, θω.
ι γιώτα: "ee", but short. Ss: ι, λι, χι.
Soft χ: T writes χι, χε. Before ε or ι, χ moves forward in the mouth, close to the "h" in "huge." Compare έχω (hard) with έχει (soft).
σ / ς σίγμα: always "s." Use σ at the start or middle of a word and ς at the end. Write λες on the board and circle the ς.
α άλφα: "a" in "father", but short. Ss: α, λα, θα, σα.
ει: T writes ε + ι = ει. The pair is said "ee", just like ι.
Punctuation: in Greek, ; is the question mark.

Reading ladder:
`θα – θε – σι – λα – χι`
`θέλω · θέλεις · θέλει`
`έχω · έχεις · έχει`
`λέω · λες · λέει · αλλά`

**0:25–0:42 Anchor verb: θέλω**
Step 1: listen. T acts as host with a tray of real drinks or picture cards.
T: Θέλω νερό. Θέλω τσάι. (Pretends to drink.) Θέλω ένα γλυκό! (rubs stomach)
Step 2: pattern on the board.

| | έχω | θέλω |
|---|---|---|
| I | έχω | θέλω |
| you | έχεις | θέλεις |
| he/she | έχει | θέλει |

T (English): Same three endings for both verbs: -ω, -εις, -ει. Learn the pattern once and use it for both.

Step 3: choral drill, then a pointing drill. T points to self, a student, then a third person. Ss call out the right form (θέλω / θέλεις / θέλει).
Step 4: questions.
T: Θέλεις λίγο νερό;
S1: Ναι, θέλω λίγο νερό, *ευχαριστώ.
T: Θέλεις ένα γλυκό;
S2: Όχι, *ευχαριστώ.
Step 5: third person.
T: (points to S1) Ο Νίκος θέλει τσάι. (points to S2) Η Μαρία θέλει κρασί.
Ss report on each other: Ο/Η ___ θέλει ___.

**0:42–0:54 Scenario: a guest at a Greek home**
Pairs take turns as host and guest. The host goes through the tray and the guest accepts or declines.
Host: Θέλεις νερό;
Guest: Ναι, θέλω λίγο νερό, *ευχαριστώ.
Host: Θέλεις ένα γλυκό;
Guest: Όχι, *ευχαριστώ.
Host: Έχεις ψωμί; (the host's partner checks the "kitchen" cards) Ναι, έχω! / Όχι.
Extension: after the role-play, each guest reports to the class: Ο ___ θέλει τσάι, αλλά… (stop there. "But…" builds suspense for next week).

**0:54–1:00 Wrap-up**
Reading card (fully decodable): `Θέλεις; – Θέλω. / Έχεις; – Έχω. / Λες; / Θέλει, αλλά…`
Exit ticket: one true sentence with θέλω and one with έχω.

**Homework:** write θ ι σ ς α ει; read the ladder aloud; record yourself offering three drinks to someone at home.

**Watch-list:** θ said as "t" or "f"; ει read as "ay"; questions said with flat intonation.

### Lesson 3: Μ · Π · Ο · Ρ | μπορώ

**At a glance**
- Letters: Μ μ /m/ · Π π /p/ · Ο ο /o/ · Ρ ρ /r/ (tapped)
- Digraph: μπ = "b" (at the start of a word)
- Readable: μπορώ, μπορείς, μπορεί, ώρα, μέρα, πόσο, πολλά, όλα, χαρά, χέρι, πρωί, όχι, Μαρία, Σάμος, Πάρος, Χίος
- Anchor verb: μπορώ (I can / may)
- New words (oral): σήμερα, αύριο, το πρωί, το βράδυ, βέβαια, λυπάμαι, ώρα, μέρα
- Keystone sentence (oral): Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ.

**0:00–0:10 Spaced review (Lessons 2, 1)**
Tray round: Θέλεις τσάι; – Ναι, θέλω. / Όχι, *ευχαριστώ.
Bag round: Έχεις ένα κλειδί;
Speed-read cards: έχει · θέλεις · αλλά · λέει.

**0:10–0:25 Letters and sounds**
μ μι: "m." Ss: μα, με, μω.
π πι: "p" with no puff of air. Hold a strip of paper in front of your lips. English "p" makes it move, and Greek π shouldn't. Ss: πα, πο.
ο όμικρον: the same sound as ω. Greek has two letters for one "o" sound, and spelling decides which.
ρ ρο: a single tap of the tongue. Americans already make this sound: it's the middle of "butter" or "ladder" said fast. Ss: ρα, ρο, ρε. Then roll a little more for emphasis: ώρα.
μπ: T writes μ + π = μπ. At the start of a word this says "b." Greek has no single letter for the "b" sound (next week shows why).

Reading ladder:
`μα – πο – ρω – μπο`
`μπορώ · μπορείς · μπορεί`
`ώρα · μέρα · πόσο · πολλά · όλα · χέρι · πρωί`
Island bonus: `Σάμος · Πάρος · Χίος` (χ is soft before ι.)

**0:25–0:42 Anchor verb: μπορώ**
Step 1: permission (gesture-heavy). T stands at the door, mimes knocking. Μπορώ; Then points at a chair. Μπορώ; Then reaches for a student's pen. Μπορώ;
Ss answer (taught as a chunk): Ναι, βέβαια.
Step 2: asking for help. T struggles with a jar lid, hands it to S1. Μπορείς;
S1 opens it. T: Μπορεί! (to the class, pointing at S1)
Step 3: pattern.

| | μπορώ |
|---|---|
| I | μπορώ |
| you | μπορείς |
| he/she | μπορεί |

T (English): Same endings as θέλω, -εις and -ει, but the stress jumps to the end: μπο-ΡΩ, μπο-ΡΕΙΣ, μπο-ΡΕΙ.

Step 4: the keystone sentence (oral). T writes the readable part: Θέλω, αλλά… and says the whole thing: Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ.
T (English): "I want to, but I can't." δεν = not. You'll read δ in two weeks, but you can say it today.
Ss repeat it with a sad face. They'll use it all year.

Step 5: time words. T writes a week strip on the board. σήμερα · αύριο · το πρωί · το βράδυ.
T: Μπορείς σήμερα;
S1: Ναι, μπορώ. / Λυπάμαι, δεν μπορώ.

**0:42–0:54 Scenario: making plans**
Each student gets a mini-calendar card with two free slots. Pairs try to find a time.
S1: Μπορείς σήμερα το βράδυ;
S2: Λυπάμαι, δεν μπορώ. Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ.
S1: Αύριο το πρωί;
S2: Ναι, αύριο το πρωί μπορώ!
Pairs that match write their time on the board (in Greek where they can).

**0:54–1:00 Wrap-up**
Reading card: `Μπορώ; – Ναι. / Μαρία, μπορείς; / Πόσο θέλεις; – Πολλά! / Έχεις ώρα; – Όχι.`
Exit ticket: say the keystone sentence plus one μπορώ sentence about tomorrow.

**Homework:** read the ladder; practice μπ = b; use "Μπορώ;" with family at least 3 times this week (taking a seat, food, the remote).

**Watch-list:** a strong aspirated π; ρ said as an American "r"; μπορώ stressed on the first syllable.

### Lesson 4: Β · Ν · Τ · Κ | βλέπω

**At a glance**
- Letters: Β β /v/ · Ν ν /n/ · Τ τ /t/ · Κ κ /k/
- Digraph: αι = /e/
- Readable: βλέπω, βλέπεις, βλέπει, βιβλίο, νερό, κρασί, κινητό, σπίτι, τι, το, τον, τα, να, και, καλά, παρακαλώ, τώρα, κάτι, τίποτα, ένα, τέλεια, θέλετε
- Anchor verb: βλέπω
- New words (oral, picture-based): θάλασσα (η), βουνό (το), ήλιος (ο), βάρκα (η), εκκλησία (η), σπίτι (το)
- Grammar idea: question words with Τι; Plus the three articles as chunks: ο/τον (masc.), η/τη (fem.), το (neut.)

**0:00–0:10 Spaced review (Lessons 3, 2)**
Plan-maker speed round: Μπορείς αύριο; – Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ.
Tray: Θέλεις λίγο νερό;
Show μπ, ει, θ cards and have Ss say each sound.

**0:10–0:25 Letters and sounds**
β βήτα: "v." Always. This is why Greek needs μπ for "b." Ss: βα, βε, βλε.
ν νι: "n." Ss: να, νε, νω.
τ ταυ: "t" with no puff, tongue against the back of the top teeth. Paper-strip test again. Ss: τα, τι, το.
κ κάπα: "k" with no puff. Before ε/ι it moves forward, a little like "ky" (κι). Ss: κα, κο, κι.
αι: T writes α + ι = αι, said "e" as in "pet." So και ("and") sounds like "keh."

Reading ladder:
`βα – νε – τι – κα – και`
`βλέπω · βλέπεις · βλέπει`
`βιβλίο · νερό · κρασί · κινητό · σπίτι`
`Τι κάνεις; – Καλά. · παρακαλώ · τώρα · τίποτα`

Students can now read whole sentences:
`Έχω ένα βιβλίο. · Θέλω νερό, παρακαλώ. · Τι βλέπεις; · Μπορείς τώρα;`

**0:25–0:42 Anchor verb: βλέπω**
Step 1: postcards. T shows large photos of a Greek island village.
T: Βλέπω τη θάλασσα. Βλέπω ένα βουνό. Βλέπω τον ήλιο. Βλέπω μια βάρκα. Βλέπω μια εκκλησία.
Step 2: pattern. βλέπω / βλέπεις / βλέπει. This is the same pattern as θέλω, so students should spot it themselves. Ask: Who sees the pattern?
Step 3: articles as chunks (color code on the board):
- blue = ο / τον (ήλιος → τον ήλιο)
- red = η / τη (θάλασσα → τη θάλασσα)
- green = το (βουνό → το βουνό)
T (English): Don't memorize rules yet. Learn each noun with its color.
Step 4: the four Τι questions. T writes:
`Τι έχεις; · Τι θέλεις; · Τι μπορείς; · Τι βλέπεις;`
Pair drill: one student asks a random question from the list, and the partner answers with any true sentence.

**0:42–0:54 Scenario A: "Τι βλέπεις;" postcard description (6 min)**
S1 holds a postcard hidden. S2 asks: Τι βλέπεις;
S1: Βλέπω τη θάλασσα και ένα σπίτι.
S2 sketches it, and they compare.

**Scenario B: first fully readable café dialogue (6 min)**
Every word is decodable except the starred chunk:
> Α: Τι θέλετε;
> Β: Ένα νερό και ένα κρασί, παρακαλώ.
> Α: Τίποτα άλλο;
> Β: Όχι, τίποτα. *Ευχαριστώ.

T (English): θέλετε = "you want," for a group or to be polite. A server would say it this way.
Pairs read, then swap roles, then replace the drinks with other items.

**0:54–1:00 Wrap-up**
Exit ticket: each student reads one line of the café dialogue aloud cold, then makes one true βλέπω sentence about the room.

**Homework:** read the dialogue aloud daily; describe one photo on your phone each day with βλέπω; write the four Τι questions.

**Watch-list:** β said as "b" (the most common error); και said as "kai"; aspirated τ and κ.

### Lesson 5: Η · Υ · Δ · Γ | negation with δεν

**At a glance**
- Letters: Η η /i/ · Υ υ /i/ · Δ δ /ð/ · Γ γ /ɣ/, /j/
- Digraphs: ου = /u/ · οι = /i/
- Readable: δεν, εδώ, εκεί, δύο, γάλα, γιατί, σήμερα, *Καλημέρα (now fully readable), η, την, πολύ, εσύ, μου, σου, του, πού, που, είναι, τουαλέτα, οι, δουλειά, Γεια σου (now readable)
- Focus: all four anchor verbs in the negative. Plus Μπορώ να δω; as a chunk
- New nouns: πορτοφόλι (το), γυαλιά (τα), τσάντα (η), κλειδιά (τα)

**0:00–0:10 Spaced review (Lessons 4, 3, 1)**
Postcard quick-fire: Τι βλέπεις;
Plans: Μπορείς αύριο το βράδυ;
Bag: Έχεις ένα στυλό; (Lesson 1 resurfaces as planned.)

**0:10–0:25 Letters and sounds**
η ήτα and υ ύψιλον: both said "ee."
"Five ways to write ee" chart on the board: ι · η · υ · ει · οι. Same sound, different spellings. Spelling is a memory job, not a pronunciation job.
δ δέλτα: "th" as in "this." Hand on throat: θ is silent (no buzz), δ buzzes. Minimal pair drill: θ… δ… θ… δ.
γ γάμμα, two sounds:
- before α, ο, ου, or a consonant: a soft gargle, the voiced version of χ (γάλα).
- before ε, ι, or ει: "y" as in "yes" (γεια = "ya", γιατί = ya-TEE).
ου: "oo" as in "food." μου, σου, πού.
οι: "ee" again (add it to the chart).

Reading ladder:
`δε – δω – γα – γε – μου – σου – πού`
`δεν έχω · δεν θέλω · δεν μπορώ · δεν βλέπω`
`Καλημέρα! · Γεια σου! · εδώ · εκεί · γιατί · είναι`

**0:25–0:42 Anchor work: negation + possession**
Step 1: δεν in front of any verb.
T: Έχω νερό. → Δεν έχω νερό. Θέλω κρασί. → Δεν θέλω κρασί.
T says positive sentences and Ss flip them negative. Then reverse.
Step 2: the keystone sentence, now readable. Board: `Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ.` Ss read it, the first time with every letter known.
Step 3: μου / σου go after the noun.
T: (holds own phone) Το κινητό μου. (points at a student's) Το κινητό σου.
Ss point and label: Η τσάντα μου. Τα κλειδιά σου.
Step 4: Γιατί; (Why?)
T: Γιατί δεν μπορείς;
S1: Γιατί… (repeats any reason, gestures fine) …έχω δουλειά.
Step 5: the chunk Μπορώ να δω; ("May I see?")
T (English): βλέπω is ongoing seeing. For a one-time "see," Greek uses δω after να. Don't analyze it. Just learn Μπορώ να δω; as one block. It's one of the most useful shop phrases there is.

**0:42–0:54 Scenario: "I can't find my phone"**
T hides students' items (with permission) or uses picture cards around the room.
S1: Πού είναι το κινητό μου; Δεν βλέπω το κινητό μου!
S2: Δεν ξέρω… (oral chunk; ξ comes next week) …Εδώ είναι!
S1: *Ευχαριστώ!
Second mini-scene, in a shop:
S1: Καλημέρα! Μπορώ να δω;
S2: Ναι, βέβαια.
S1: Πόσο κάνει;
S2: Δύο *ευρώ.

**0:54–1:00 Wrap-up**
Exit ticket: say one negative sentence with each of the four anchor verbs.

**Homework:** add η, υ, οι to your "ee" chart; read the ladder; label five things at home with sticky notes, e.g. το κινητό μου.

**Watch-list:** δ said as "d"; γ said as English "g"; ου said as "oh-oo."

### Lesson 6: Φ · Ζ · Ξ · Ψ | the alphabet is complete

**At a glance**
- Letters: Φ φ /f/ · Ζ ζ /z/ · Ξ ξ /ks/ · Ψ ψ /ps/
- Digraphs: αυ/ευ · ντ · γκ/γγ · τσ · τζ
- Readable: everything. ευχαριστώ, αυτό, αύριο, ξέρω, ψωμί, φίλος, καφές, τσάι, τζατζίκι, ντομάτα, φαγητό, έξι
- Focus: consolidation of the four anchor verbs (positive, negative, question). Bonus chunk: Δεν ξέρω.
- Celebration: say the whole alphabet in order and spell names

**0:00–0:10 Spaced review (Lessons 5, 4, 2)**
Lost-item scene, reversed roles.
Café dialogue from Lesson 4, read from memory.
Tray from Lesson 2: Θέλεις λίγο γάλα;

**0:10–0:25 Letters and sounds**
φ φι: "f." ζ ζήτα: "z." ξ ξι: "ks" as in "taxi." ψ ψι: "ps," even at the start of a word. ψωμί is pso-MEE, not "so-mee."
αυ / ευ:
- "av / ev" before a vowel or a voiced consonant: αύριο = AV-rio (now readable, after weeks of saying it).
- "af / ef" before a voiceless consonant (κ, π, τ, χ, θ, σ, φ, ξ, ψ): αυτό = af-TO, ευχαριστώ = ef-ha-ri-STO.
ντ = "d" (ντομάτα). γκ / γγ = "g" (γκαρσόνι). τσ = "ts" (τσάι). τζ = "dz" (τζατζίκι).
Starred chunks no longer need stars: Ss read ευχαριστώ for real.

Alphabet in order (chant it, then sing it if your group likes that):
`Α Β Γ Δ Ε Ζ Η Θ Ι Κ Λ Μ Ν Ξ Ο Π Ρ Σ Τ Υ Φ Χ Ψ Ω`
Each student spells their own name in Greek letters on a name card.

**0:25–0:42 Anchor consolidation: the verb grid**
Board grid. Every sentence in it comes back in the reading below. Teams race to fill it out loud:

| | + | – | ? |
|---|---|---|---|
| έχω | Έχω ώρα. | Δεν έχω ώρα. | Έχεις ώρα; |
| θέλω | Θέλω έναν καφέ. | Δεν θέλω καφέ. | Θέλεις έναν καφέ; |
| μπορώ | Μπορώ αύριο. | Δεν μπορώ αύριο. | Μπορείς αύριο; |
| βλέπω | Βλέπω τη Μαρία. | Δεν βλέπω τη Μαρία. | Βλέπεις τη Μαρία; |

Then: T calls out a verb, a form, and a person ("θέλω, question, you!"), and Ss build the sentence on the spot.
Bonus: Ξέρεις; – Δεν ξέρω. (shrug). Students will use it constantly.

**0:42–0:54 First reading: "Στο καφενείο" (fully decodable)**
> Ο Νίκος είναι στο καφενείο. Βλέπει τη Μαρία.
> — Γεια σου, Μαρία! Τι κάνεις;
> — Καλά, εσύ;
> — Καλά. Θέλεις έναν καφέ;
> — Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ. Δεν έχω ώρα σήμερα.
> — Αύριο μπορείς;
> — Αύριο το πρωί μπορώ.
> — Τέλεια! Τα λέμε αύριο.

1. T reads aloud while Ss follow along. 2. Choral read. 3. Pairs read, swapping roles. 4. Pairs perform it with their own names and a new day or time.
Teaching notes: έναν καφέ is masculine (ο καφές). Τα λέμε = "See you" (literally "we'll say them").

**0:54–1:00 Celebration and check**
Phase A checkpoint: each student reads two lines of the story aloud and says one true sentence with each anchor verb. Note who struggles with which sounds, and target those in Lesson 7–8 warm-ups.

**Homework:** read the story daily for 5 days and record yourself on day 1 and day 5 so you can hear the difference; spell three family members' names in Greek.

### Lesson 7: Introductions and Christmas | + έχω/θέλω

Week of Dec 14 · Theme: Who am I / Christmas, κάλαντα · Grammar: είμαι (sg), από

**At a glance**
- New words: είμαι, είσαι, είναι, από, εδώ, Αμερική, Ελλάδα, Καλά Χριστούγεννα
- Structure: είμαι (singular) + από + place
- Anchor recycling: Έχω μια οικογένεια εδώ. Θέλω να δω την Ελλάδα.
- Materials: a world map, name cards, a small triangle (τρίγωνο) or a bell, a bag of wrapped sweets
- Review from lessons: 6, 5, 3

**0:00–0:10 Spaced review (Lessons 6, 5, 3)**
T says the English, and Ss answer in Greek. Students mark each item red, yellow, or green on the packet.

- L6: Do you want a coffee? → Θέλεις έναν καφέ;
- L6: Fine, and you? → Καλά, εσύ;
- L5: I can't see… / I don't see… → Δεν βλέπω…
- L5: Here it is! → Εδώ είναι!
- L3: Can you make it tonight? → Μπορείς σήμερα το βράδυ;
- L3: Sorry, I can't. → Λυπάμαι, δεν μπορώ.
- Then: any red words from last week's packet.

**0:10–0:25 Warm-up: new language and anchor work**
Guess first (30 sec): "How do you think you'd say 'I'm … from …'?" Take two or three guesses, then teach Είμαι ο/η… από….

**Step 1: listen (είμαι)**
T: (hand on chest) Είμαι ο/η [name]. Είμαι από την Αμερική.
T: (points at Greece on the map) Η Μαρία είναι από την Ελλάδα. Ο Νίκος είναι από την Κύπρο.
T: (to S1) Εσύ; Από πού είσαι;
S1: Είμαι από την Αμερική.
T (English): είμαι = I am, είσαι = you are, είναι = he, she, or it is. Greek puts the article before a name: ο Νίκος, η Μαρία. So a man says Είμαι ο…, and a woman says Είμαι η….
**Step 2: the είμαι grid**

Board: the είμαι grid:

| | + | – | ? |
|---|---|---|---|
| I am | Είμαι εδώ. | Δεν είμαι εδώ. | |
| you are | Είσαι εδώ. | Δεν είσαι εδώ. | Είσαι εδώ; |
| he, she is | Η Μαρία είναι εδώ. | Η Μαρία δεν είναι εδώ. | Η Μαρία είναι εδώ; |
| with από | Είμαι από την Αμερική. | Δεν είμαι από την Ελλάδα. | Από πού είσαι; |

T: (points to a person, then a place on the map) Ss build the sentence: Ο Νίκος είναι από την Κρήτη.
**Step 3: anchor recycling**
T: Έχω μια οικογένεια εδώ. Θέλω να δω την Ελλάδα.
S1: Έχω μια οικογένεια εδώ. Θέλω να δω την ___.
**Step 4: Christmas and the κάλαντα**
T: Καλά Χριστούγεννα! — Ss: Καλά Χριστούγεννα!
T: Χαίρω πολύ! — Ss: Χαίρω πολύ! (Nice to meet you.)
T (English): On Christmas Eve morning, children go door to door with a triangle and sing the κάλαντα. The family gives them coins or sweets.
T: (sings, line by line, with the triangle) Καλήν εσπέραν, άρχοντες… — Ss echo (oral only, don't analyze it).
T: (hands out sweets) Ορίστε! — Ss: Ευχαριστούμε! Καλά Χριστούγεννα!
**Warm-up dialogue: meeting at the church Christmas party**
A: Γεια σου! Είμαι η Ελένη.
B: Γεια σου, Ελένη! Είμαι ο Νίκος. Από πού είσαι;
A: Είμαι από την Αμερική. Εσύ;
B: Είμαι από την Ελλάδα, αλλά έχω μια οικογένεια εδώ.
A: Καλά Χριστούγεννα!
B: Καλά Χριστούγεννα!

Gestures (students make each one themselves as they say the phrase):
- Είμαι ο/η… από…: Hand on chest, then point to the place on the map.
- Από πού είσαι;: Point at the partner, then sweep the hand over the map.
- Είμαι από την Αμερική.: Hand on chest, then point west.
- Έχω μια οικογένεια εδώ.: Arms in a hug-circle, then point down ("here").
- Καλά Χριστούγεννα!: Mime ringing the κάλαντα triangle.

Recall 1 (1 min): papers face-down. T gives the English for three of today's phrases; Ss answer in Greek, with the gesture.

**0:25–0:40 Main scene**
Read it once in pairs, swap roles, then perform it without the paper.

**Who are you? and Christmas wishes.** Introductions at a Christmas gathering. (The κάλαντα are sung in the warm-up.)

A: Γεια σας! Είμαι η Μαρία. — Hello! I'm Maria.
B: Χαίρω πολύ. Είμαι ο Νίκος. — Nice to meet you. I'm Nikos.
A: Από πού είσαι, Νίκο; — Where are you from, Nikos?
B: Είμαι από την Αμερική. Εσύ; — I'm from America. You?
A: Είμαι από την Ελλάδα, από την Κρήτη. — I'm from Greece, from Crete.
B: Είσαι εδώ με την οικογένεια; — Are you here with your family?
A: Ναι, έχω μια οικογένεια εδώ. — Yes, I have a family here.
B: Τι ωραία! Θέλεις να δεις την Αμερική; — How nice! Do you want to see America?
A: Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ τώρα. — I want to, but I can't right now.
B: Καλά Χριστούγεννα, Μαρία! — Merry Christmas, Maria!
A: Καλά Χριστούγεννα! — Merry Christmas!
T: Από πού είναι ο Νίκος; — Where is Nikos from?
Ss: Είναι από την Αμερική. — He's from America.
T: Και η Μαρία; — And Maria?
Ss: Είναι από την Ελλάδα, από την Κρήτη. — She's from Greece, from Crete.

**0:40–0:54 Fork it**
Slot swaps (change one per replay):
- Who: ο/η + your name · a famous person · a family member
- From: από την Αμερική · από την Ελλάδα · από την Κρήτη · από εδώ
- Wish: Καλά Χριστούγεννα! · Καλή χρονιά! · Χρόνια πολλά!

Twists:
- Name tags are swapped. Everyone is someone else and has to introduce themselves in character.
- Mingle: each student has to find someone "from" each place on a card, and report back with Ο/Η ___ είναι από…

Every lesson (papers face-down for every replay):
- Role swap: A and B trade parts.
- Person shift: ask about someone else (Έχει…; Θέλει…;).
- Tile round (can replace the slot-swap replay): pairs build three scene sentences from the Lesson 7 tiles in the textbook, on the sentence mat. Then flip to a question, add δεν, swap one tile, and write the last version by hand.
- Retell round (last replay): each student retells the scene to a new partner three times, with less time each time (60, 45, then 30 seconds). The listener only listens.
- Mix-in: work one review line into a replay, for example Θέλεις έναν καφέ;

A good pattern is three replays: the first straight, the second with one slot swap, and the third as the retell round.
Recall 2 (1 min): papers face-down. T gives the English for three warm-up phrases not used in Recall 1; Ss answer in Greek, with the gesture.

**0:54–1:00 Wrap-up**
Recall 3 · Exit ticket: Είμαι ο/η ___ από ___. Papers away. Ss write it by hand on an index card, then say it.

**Homework (10 min/day)**
1. Say your introduction aloud once a day, and record it on day 1.
2. Learn the first two lines of the κάλαντα with a recording.
3. Write three sentences: Η μητέρα μου είναι από…, Ο πατέρας μου είναι από…, Είμαι από…

**Out in the world:** Find a Greek Orthodox church, Greek festival, or Greek club near you and look up when it meets. If you can, go and say Καλά Χριστούγεννα or Γεια σας to one person.

**Watch-list:** είναι is EE-ne (stress on the first syllable). Students drop the article before names. την stays as την before a vowel: την Αμερική, την Ελλάδα.

Intermediate students: use the longer scene "New in the parish: work, roots, and Christmas customs" (in the vault, Scripts/Intermediate) during the main-scene block.

### Lesson 8: Vasilopita - who has the coin | έχω

**At a glance**

T: (cuts) Ένα κομμάτι για τον Χριστό. Ένα κομμάτι για το σπίτι. Ένα κομμάτι για τη Μαρία…
T: Πόσα κομμάτια; — Ss: (count together) Ένα, δύο, τρία… δέκα!

A: Καλή χρονιά!
B: Καλή χρονιά! Θέλεις ένα κομμάτι;
A: Ναι, ευχαριστώ.
B: Ποιος έχει το φλουρί;
A: Δεν έχω. Εσύ;
C: Εγώ! Εγώ έχω το φλουρί!
All: Μπράβο! Καλή χρονιά!

Gestures (students make each one themselves as they say the phrase):
- Ποιος έχει το φλουρί;: Hold up an imaginary coin and look around the room.
- Εγώ έχω!: Jump up, coin held high.
- ένα κομμάτι: Slice the air like a knife cutting cake.
- Καλή χρονιά!: Raise a glass for a toast.

Recall 1 (1 min): papers face-down. T gives the English for three of today's phrases; Ss answer in Greek, with the gesture.

**0:25–0:40 Main scene**
Read it once in pairs, swap roles, then perform it without the paper.

**Cutting the vasilopita.** The family cuts the New Year's cake. Count the pieces and find out who has the coin.

T: Καλή χρονιά! — Happy New Year!
Ss: Καλή χρονιά! — Happy New Year!

T: Ένα κομμάτι για τη Μαρία. — One piece for Maria.
Maria: Ευχαριστώ! — Thank you!

Nikos: Δεν έχω! — I don't!
Grandma: Εγώ! Εγώ έχω το φλουρί! — Me! I have the coin!

Slot swaps (change one per replay):
- Number: ένα · δύο · τρία · τέσσερα · πέντε · έξι · επτά · οκτώ · εννιά · δέκα
- For whom: για τη μαμά · για τον μπαμπά · για τη γιαγιά · για τον παππού
- Wish: Καλή χρονιά! · Χρόνια πολλά! · Ευτυχισμένο το νέο έτος!

Twists:
- Really hide a coin under one of ten cups. Each student counts out loud to choose a cup.
- Countdown: count backwards from ten together, then shout Καλή χρονιά!

Every lesson (papers face-down for every replay):
- Role swap: A and B trade parts.
- Person shift: ask about someone else (Έχει…; Θέλει…;).
- Tile round (can replace the slot-swap replay): pairs build three scene sentences from the Lesson 8 tiles in the textbook, on the sentence mat. Then flip to a question, add δεν, swap one tile, and write the last version by hand.
- Retell round (last replay): each student retells the scene to a new partner three times, with less time each time (60, 45, then 30 seconds). The listener only listens.
- Mix-in: work one review line into a replay, for example Είμαι ο/η… από…

Recall 3 · Exit ticket: Count from δέκα down to ένα, then ask one Ποιος έχει…; question. Papers away. Ss write it by hand on an index card, then say it.

1. Count objects at home in Greek (stairs, plates, shoes).
2. Say your phone number digit by digit in Greek.
3. Wish three people Καλή χρονιά! this week.

**Out in the world:** Bake or buy any round cake, hide a coin in it, and cut it the Greek way for your family. Count the pieces out loud in Greek.

### Lesson 9: My family | all 4 plural

Week of Jan 11 · Theme: My family · Grammar: plurals: έχουμε/έχετε/έχουν

**At a glance**
- New words: μητέρα, πατέρας, αδελφός, αδελφή, παιδί/παιδιά, γιαγιά, παππούς
- Structure: all four anchor verbs in the plural: έχουμε / έχετε / έχουν
- Anchor recycling: Έχουμε δύο παιδιά. Θέλουν…
- Materials: family photos (ask students to bring 2–3), a family tree drawn on the board
- Review from lessons: 8, 7, 5, 1

**0:00–0:10 Spaced review (Lessons 8, 7, 5, 1)**
T says the English, and Ss answer in Greek. Students mark each item red, yellow, or green on the packet.

- L8: Who has the coin? → Ποιος έχει το φλουρί;
- L8: I do! / I have it! → Εγώ έχω!
- L7: I'm from America. → Είμαι από την Αμερική.
- L7: I have a family here. → Έχω μια οικογένεια εδώ.
- L5: Why can't you? → Γιατί δεν μπορείς;
- L5: I have work. / I'm busy. → Έχω δουλειά.
- L1: Yes, I do. → Ναι, έχω.
- L1: What do you have? → Τι έχεις;
- Then: any red words from last week's packet.

**0:10–0:25 Warm-up: new language and anchor work**
Guess first (30 sec): "How do you think you'd say 'We have two children.'?" Take two or three guesses, then teach Έχουμε δύο παιδιά..

**Step 1: family words**
T: (points at the tree) Αυτή είναι η μητέρα μου. Αυτός είναι ο πατέρας μου.
T: Η γιαγιά μου. Ο παππούς μου. Ο αδελφός μου. Η αδελφή μου.
T: (holds up a photo) Μια φωτογραφία. Έχω έναν αδελφό. Έχεις αδελφό ή αδελφή;
T: Ένα παιδί… δύο παιδιά.
T (English): αυτός is for a man, αυτή is for a woman. Many Greeks say αδερφός and αδερφή. Both are fine.
**Step 2: we, you all, they**
T (English): So far we've had I, you, and he or she. The plural adds three endings: -ουμε (we), -ετε (you all, or polite you), -ουν (they).

Board: the plural endings (-ουμε, -ετε, -ουν):

| I | we | you all / polite | they |
|---|---|---|---|
| έχω | έχουμε | έχετε | έχουν |
| θέλω | θέλουμε | θέλετε | θέλουν |
| βλέπω | βλέπουμε | βλέπετε | βλέπουν |
| μπορώ | μπορούμε | μπορείτε | μπορούν |

T: (gestures to self and S1) Εμείς έχουμε βιβλία. (to the group) Εσείς έχετε βιβλία; (points at two others) Αυτοί έχουν νερό.
**Step 3: flip drill**
T: Εγώ έχω. — Ss: Εμείς έχουμε.
T: Θέλεις. — Ss: Θέλετε.
T: Μπορεί. — Ss: Μπορούν.
**Step 4: personalize**
T: Έχετε παιδιά; — S1: Ναι, έχουμε δύο παιδιά. / Όχι, δεν έχουμε.
T: Τι θέλουν τα παιδιά; — S1: Θέλουν γλυκά!
**Warm-up dialogue: the family photo**
A: Αυτή είναι η οικογένειά μου.
B: Τι ωραία! Ποιος είναι αυτός;
A: Ο αδελφός μου. Και αυτή είναι η γιαγιά μου.
B: Έχετε παιδιά;
A: Ναι, έχουμε δύο παιδιά.
B: Και τι θέλουν τα παιδιά;
A: Θέλουν έναν σκύλο! Αλλά δεν μπορούμε.

Gestures (students make each one themselves as they say the phrase):
- Έχουμε δύο παιδιά.: Circle hand between yourself and a partner ("we"), then show two short heights.
- Έχετε παιδιά;: Sweep a hand across the group, then show a child's height.
- η γιαγιά μου: Bend over a pretend cane, hand on your chest.
- ο παππούς μου: Twirl a pretend moustache, or play with κομπολόι worry beads.
- Θέλουν…: Point at two other people, then reach out eagerly.

Recall 1 (1 min): papers face-down. T gives the English for three of today's phrases; Ss answer in Greek, with the gesture.

**0:25–0:40 Main scene**
Read it once in pairs, swap roles, then perform it without the paper.

**Family photos.** Two friends show each other family photos. This is the first time all six present-tense forms appear together.

A: Αυτή είναι η οικογένειά μου. — This is my family.
B: Ωραία! Αυτή είναι η μητέρα σου; — Nice! Is this your mother?
A: Ναι, και αυτός είναι ο πατέρας μου. — Yes, and this is my father.
B: Έχεις αδελφό ή αδελφή; — Do you have a brother or a sister?
A: Έχω έναν αδελφό και μια αδελφή. — I have a brother and a sister.
B: Έχουν παιδιά; — Do they have kids?
A: Ναι, έχουν τρία παιδιά. Εσείς; Έχετε παιδιά; — Yes, they have three kids. And you? Do you have kids?
B: Ναι, έχουμε δύο παιδιά. Θέλουν γλυκά! — Yes, we have two kids. They want sweets!
A: Και η γιαγιά σου; — And your grandmother?
B: Η γιαγιά μου και ο παππούς μου είναι στην Ελλάδα. — My grandmother and grandfather are in Greece.
A: Βλέπετε τη γιαγιά; — Do you see your grandmother?
B: Όχι, δεν μπορούμε. Αλλά έχουμε φωτογραφίες! — No, we can't. But we have photos!
T: Εμείς έχουμε… εσείς έχετε… αυτοί έχουν… — We have… you all have… they have…

Slot swaps (change one per replay):
- Who: η μητέρα · ο πατέρας · ο αδελφός · η αδελφή · η γιαγιά · ο παππούς

- Tile round (can replace the slot-swap replay): pairs build three scene sentences from the Lesson 9 tiles in the textbook, on the sentence mat. Then flip to a question, add δεν, swap one tile, and write the last version by hand.
- Retell round (last replay): each student retells the scene to a new partner three times, with less time each time (90, 60, then 45 seconds). The listener only listens.
- Mix-in: work one review line into a replay, for example Ποιος έχει το φλουρί;

A good pattern is three replays: the first straight, the second with one slot swap, and the third as the retell round.
Recall 2 (1 min): papers face-down. T gives the English for three warm-up phrases not used in Recall 1; Ss answer in Greek, with the gesture.

**0:54–1:00 Wrap-up**
Recall 3 · Exit ticket: One sentence with έχουμε and one with θέλουν. Papers away. Ss write it by hand on an index card, then say it.

### Lesson 10: My day | θέλω, μπορώ

Week of Jan 18 · Theme: My day · Grammar: κάνω; θέλω να + verb

**At a glance**
- New words: κάνω, δουλεύω, σπίτι, πρωί / μεσημέρι / βράδυ, μετά, πάντα
- Structure: θέλω να + verb; μπορώ να + verb
- Anchor recycling: Θέλω να κάνω… αλλά δεν μπορώ.
- Materials: three picture cards (sun rising, sun high, moon), a paper clock
- Review from lessons: 9, 8, 6, 2

**0:00–0:10 Spaced review (Lessons 9, 8, 6, 2)**
T says the English, and Ss answer in Greek. Students mark each item red, yellow, or green on the packet.

- L9: We have two children. → Έχουμε δύο παιδιά.
- L9: Do you have children? → Έχετε παιδιά;
- L8: a piece → ένα κομμάτι
- L8: Happy New Year! → Καλή χρονιά!
- L6: Perfect! → Τέλεια!
- L6: See you tomorrow. → Τα λέμε αύριο.
- L2: …but… → …αλλά…
- L2: Would you like a little water? → Θέλεις λίγο νερό;
- Then: any red words from last week's packet.

**0:10–0:25 Warm-up: new language and anchor work**
Guess first (30 sec): "How do you think you'd say 'I want to do…, but I can't.'?" Take two or three guesses, then teach Θέλω να κάνω…, αλλά δεν μπορώ..

**Step 1: my day in three pictures**
T: (sunrise card) Το πρωί δουλεύω. (sun high) Το μεσημέρι είμαι στο σπίτι. (moon) Το βράδυ βλέπω τηλεόραση.
T: Τι κάνεις το πρωί; — S1: Το πρωί δουλεύω.

Board: κάνω (I do, I make):

| | κάνω |
|---|---|
| I | κάνω |
| you | κάνεις |
| he, she, it | κάνει |
| we | κάνουμε |
| you all / polite | κάνετε |
| they | κάνουν |

T (English): You already know Τι κάνεις; as "How are you?" It also means "What do you do?" or "What are you doing?"
**Step 2: θέλω να + verb**
T: Θέλω να κάνω γυμναστική, αλλά δεν μπορώ. Δουλεύω πάντα!
T (English): After να, learn the verb as one block with it. Some verbs use a special form after να, like να δω (from Lesson 5) and να πάω (next week).
Chain: S1: Θέλω να κάνω ___, αλλά δεν μπορώ. — S2: Γιατί; — S1: Γιατί δουλεύω.
**Step 3: μπορώ να + verb**
T: Μπορώ να δουλεύω από το σπίτι. Εσύ μπορείς;
S1: Ναι, μπορώ. / Όχι, δεν μπορώ.
**Step 4: μετά and πάντα**
T: Το πρωί δουλεύω. Μετά… είμαι στο σπίτι. Το βράδυ πάντα βλέπω τηλεόραση.
Pairs: tell your day in 3 sentences with μετά.
**Warm-up dialogue: a busy day**
A: Τι κάνεις το πρωί;
B: Το πρωί δουλεύω. Μετά είμαι στο σπίτι.
A: Και το βράδυ;
B: Το βράδυ θέλω να κάνω γυμναστική, αλλά δεν μπορώ.
A: Γιατί;
B: Γιατί έχω πάντα δουλειά!
A: Μπορείς να δουλεύεις από το σπίτι;
B: Ναι, αλλά στο σπίτι έχω δύο παιδιά!

Gestures (students make each one themselves as they say the phrase):
- Θέλω να κάνω…, αλλά δεν μπορώ.: Reach out eagerly, then pull back and shake your head.
- Τι κάνεις το πρωί;: Stretch as if waking, palms up.
- Μετά δουλεύω.: Hand hops forward, then mime typing.
- Μπορώ να…: Flex an arm ("I can"), then point ahead.
- στο σπίτι: Make a roof over your head with both hands.

Recall 1 (1 min): papers face-down. T gives the English for three of today's phrases; Ss answer in Greek, with the gesture.

**0:25–0:40 Main scene**
Read it once in pairs, swap roles, then perform it without the paper.

**My day.** Two friends compare their days and what they want to do but can't.

A: Τι κάνεις το πρωί; — What do you do in the morning?
B: Το πρωί πίνω καφέ. Μετά δουλεύω. — In the morning I drink coffee. Then I work.
A: Δουλεύεις στο σπίτι; — Do you work from home?
B: Ναι, δουλεύω στο σπίτι. Εσύ; Τι κάνεις το πρωί; — Yes, I work from home. You? What do you do in the morning?
A: Το πρωί είμαι στη δουλειά. — In the morning I'm at work.
B: Και το μεσημέρι; — And at midday?
A: Το μεσημέρι κάνω ένα διάλειμμα. — At midday I take a break.
B: Και το βράδυ; — And in the evening?
A: Το βράδυ θέλω να κάνω γυμναστική, αλλά δεν μπορώ. Είμαι κουρασμένη! — In the evening I want to exercise, but I can't. I'm tired!
B: Κι εγώ! Το βράδυ θέλω να διαβάσω, αλλά βλέπω τηλεόραση. — Me too! In the evening I want to read, but I watch TV.
A: Χα! Μπορούμε να κάνουμε γυμναστική μαζί; — Ha! Can we exercise together?
B: Ναι! Το πρωί μπορώ. — Yes! I can in the morning.
A: Τέλεια! Πάντα το πρωί! — Perfect! Always in the morning!

**0:40–0:54 Fork it**
Slot swaps (change one per replay):
- Time of day: το πρωί · το μεσημέρι · το απόγευμα · το βράδυ
- Activity: δουλεύω · κάνω γυμναστική · διαβάζω · βλέπω τηλεόραση · πίνω καφέ
- Wish: Θέλω να… · Μπορώ να…; · Θέλω να…, αλλά δεν μπορώ.

Twists:
- Charades: one student mimes their day, and the class narrates it in the third person (Το πρωί πίνει καφέ…).
- Excuses round: every Θέλω να… needs a reason it isn't happening (αλλά δεν μπορώ, γιατί…).

Every lesson (papers face-down for every replay):
- Role swap: A and B trade parts.
- Person shift: retell it about someone else (έχει, έχουμε, έχουν).

- Retell round (last replay): each student retells the scene to a new partner three times, with less time each time (90, 60, then 45 seconds). The listener only listens.
- Mix-in: work one review line into a replay, for example Έχουμε δύο παιδιά.

A good pattern is three replays: the first straight, the second with one slot swap, and the third as the retell round.
Recall 2 (1 min): papers face-down. T gives the English for three warm-up phrases not used in Recall 1; Ss answer in Greek, with the gesture.

**0:54–1:00 Wrap-up**
Recall 3 · Exit ticket: Θέλω να ___, αλλά ___. Papers away. Ss write it by hand on an index card, then say it.

**Homework (10 min/day)**
1. Each morning, say your day in 3 sentences: Το πρωί…, Μετά…, Το βράδυ….
2. Make one card with πρωί / μεσημέρι / βράδυ and something you do at each time.

**Out in the world:** For one day, write a short paper log of your day in Greek: Το πρωί… Το μεσημέρι… Το βράδυ… Read it aloud before bed.

Intermediate students: use the longer scene "A normal day, weekdays and weekends" (in the vault, Scripts/Intermediate) during the main-scene block.

T: (walks to each card) Πάω στη δουλειά. Πάω στο σούπερ μάρκετ. Πάω στην εκκλησία. Πάω στη θάλασσα. Πάω στο χωριό.

Board: πάω (I go):

| | πάω |
|---|---|
| I | πάω |
| you | πας |
| he, she, it | πάει |
| we | πάμε |
| you all / polite | πάτε |
| they | πάνε |

T (English): Πάμε means "we go," and also "let's go!"
**Step 2: σε + article**
Board: σε + το = στο · σε + τη(ν) = στη(ν) · σε + τον = στον
T: η θάλασσα — Ss: στη θάλασσα. T: το χωριό — Ss: στο χωριό. T: ο φούρνος — Ss: στον φούρνο. T: η εκκλησία — Ss: στην εκκλησία.
T (English): Keep the ν before a vowel and before κ, π, τ: στην εκκλησία, στην Πάτρα. Drop it before most other consonants: στη θάλασσα.
**Step 3: Πού πας;**
T: Πού πας; — S1: (walks to a card) Πάω στη δουλειά.
T: Πού πάει η Μαρία; — S2: Πάει στο σούπερ μάρκετ.
**Step 4: θέλω να πάω**
T: Θέλω να πάω στη θάλασσα. Μπορούμε να πάμε;
Ss: Ναι, πάμε! / Δεν μπορούμε, έχουμε δουλειά.
**Warm-up dialogue: Sunday plans**
A: Πού πας;
B: Πάω στο σούπερ μάρκετ. Εσύ;
A: Πάω στην εκκλησία. Μετά θέλω να πάω στη θάλασσα.
B: Στη θάλασσα; Μπορούμε να πάμε μαζί;
A: Ναι, πάμε!

Gestures (students make each one themselves as they say the phrase):
- Θέλω να πάω στη θάλασσα.: Walk your fingers forward, then make waves.
- Μπορούμε να πάμε;: Circle a hand to include the group, then wave "come on".
- Πάμε στην εκκλησία.: Wave "come on", then make the sign of the cross.
- στο χωριό: Mime shooing chickens, or picking olives.
- στη δουλειά: Mime carrying a briefcase.

Recall 1 (1 min): papers face-down. T gives the English for three of today's phrases; Ss answer in Greek, with the gesture.

**0:25–0:40 Main scene**
Read it once in pairs, swap roles, then perform it without the paper.

**Where are you going?.** Two friends run into each other and make weekend plans to go places.

A: Γεια! Πού πας; — Hi! Where are you going?
B: Πάω στη δουλειά. Εσύ; — I'm going to work. You?
A: Πάω στο σούπερ μάρκετ. Μετά πάω στο σπίτι. — I'm going to the supermarket. Then I'm going home.
B: Το Σάββατο πού πας; — Where are you going on Saturday?
A: Θέλω να πάω στη θάλασσα! — I want to go to the beach!
B: Μπορώ να έρθω; — Can I come?
A: Ναι! Πάμε μαζί! — Yes! Let's go together!
B: Και την Κυριακή; — And on Sunday?
A: Την Κυριακή πάμε στην εκκλησία, και μετά στο χωριό. — On Sunday we go to church, and then to the village.
B: Τι ωραία! Θέλω να πάω στο χωριό σου! — How nice! I want to go to your village!
A: Μπορούμε να πάμε το καλοκαίρι. — We can go in the summer.
B: Πάμε! — Let's go!
T: στο σπίτι, στο χωριό · στη θάλασσα, στη δουλειά · στον φούρνο — to the house, to the village · to the sea, to work · to the bakery

**0:40–0:54 Fork it**
Slot swaps (change one per replay):
- Place: στο σπίτι · στο σούπερ μάρκετ · στη θάλασσα · στη δουλειά · στην εκκλησία · στον φούρνο
- When: σήμερα · το Σάββατο · την Κυριακή · το καλοκαίρι
- Invite: Πάμε! · Μπορώ να έρθω; · Θέλεις να έρθεις;

Twists:
- Place cards are spread around the room. Students walk to the card as they say Πάω στο/στη/στον…
- One person keeps changing their mind about where to go, and the other has to keep up.

Every lesson (papers face-down for every replay):
- Role swap: A and B trade parts.
- Person shift: retell it about someone else (έχει, έχουμε, έχουν).
- Tile round (can replace the slot-swap replay): pairs build three scene sentences from the Lesson 11 tiles in the textbook, on the sentence mat. Then flip to a question, add δεν, swap one tile, and write the last version by hand.
- Retell round (last replay): each student retells the scene to a new partner three times, with less time each time (90, 60, then 45 seconds). The listener only listens.
- Mix-in: work one review line into a replay, for example Θέλω να κάνω…, αλλά δεν μπορώ.

A good pattern is three replays: the first straight, the second with one slot swap, and the third as the retell round.
Recall 2 (1 min): papers face-down. T gives the English for three warm-up phrases not used in Recall 1; Ss answer in Greek, with the gesture.

**0:54–1:00 Wrap-up**
Recall 3 · Exit ticket: Say where you go on Sunday: Την Κυριακή πάω στ___. Papers away. Ss write it by hand on an index card, then say it.

**Homework (10 min/day)**
1. Every time you leave the house this week, say where you're going: Πάω στ___.
2. Make a flashcard for σε + article and quiz yourself with five places.

**Out in the world:** On a printed or hand-drawn map of your town, label five places in Greek (το σπίτι, η εκκλησία, η θάλασσα…). Say Πάω στο… as you pass each one this week.

**Watch-list:** Watch for σε το (say στο). Keep the ν in στην before vowels. πάμε is PA-me, and πάνε is PA-ne.

Intermediate students: use the longer scene "Planning a road trip in the Peloponnese" (in the vault, Scripts/Intermediate) during the main-scene block.

|---|---|

T: Θα πάμε βόλτα. Θα δούμε μια ταινία. Θα φάμε μαζί.

### Lesson 22: Yesterday I wanted | all 4, past

**At a glance**
- New words: χθες, πέρσι, πριν, ήμουν (chunk)

**0:00–0:10 Spaced review (Lessons 21, 20, 18, 14)**
T says the English, and Ss answer in Greek. Students mark each item red, yellow, or green on the packet.

- L21: I like… (one thing) → Μου αρέσει…
- L21: I like… (several things) → Μου αρέσουν…

- L14: at six thirty → στις έξι και μισή

**0:10–0:25 Warm-up: new language and anchor work**
Guess first (30 sec): "How do you think you'd say 'Yesterday I wanted to go, but I couldn't.'?" Take two or three guesses, then teach Χθες ήθελα να πάω, αλλά δεν μπορούσα..

**Step 1: the four anchors in the past**

Board: the anchors now and in the past:

| Now | Past (was, used to) |
|---|---|
| έχω (I have) | είχα (I had) |
| θέλω (I want) | ήθελα (I wanted) |
| μπορώ (I can) | μπορούσα (I could) |
| βλέπω (I see) | έβλεπα (I was seeing, watching) |
| είμαι (I am) | ήμουν (I was) |

T (English): This past tense describes how things were, or what was going on. The stress moves back, and some verbs add a letter at the front (ε- or η-).

Board: past person endings:

| | είχα (had) | ήθελα (wanted) | μπορούσα (could) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | είχα | ήθελα | μπορούσα |
| you | είχες | ήθελες | μπορούσες |
| he, she, it | είχε | ήθελε | μπορούσε |
| we | είχαμε | θέλαμε | μπορούσαμε |
| you all / polite | είχατε | θέλατε | μπορούσατε |
| they | είχαν | ήθελαν | μπορούσαν |

The extra ή- of ήθελα drops in θέλαμε and θέλατε, because the stress moves off it.

**Step 2: walk the timeline backward**
T: (on "today") Σήμερα έχω ώρα. (steps back to "yesterday") Χθες δεν είχα ώρα.
T: Σήμερα μπορώ. — Ss: (step back) Χθες δεν μπορούσα.
Flip drill: T says a present sentence, and Ss step back and say it with χθες.
**Step 3: the keystone sentence in the past**
Board: Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ. → Ήθελα, αλλά δεν μπορούσα.
T: Χθες ήθελα να πάω στη θάλασσα, αλλά δεν μπορούσα. — Ss repeat, then change the place.
**Step 4: ήμουν**
T: Πού ήσουν χθες; — S1: Ήμουν στο σπίτι.
T: Πέρσι ήμουν στην Ελλάδα. Εσύ; Πού ήσουν πέρσι;
**Warm-up dialogue**
A: Πού ήσουν χθες;
B: Ήμουν στο σπίτι. Είχα πολλή δουλειά.
A: Ήθελα να σε δω!
B: Κι εγώ ήθελα, αλλά δεν μπορούσα.
A: Και το βράδυ;
B: Το βράδυ έβλεπα μια ταινία.

Gestures (students make each one themselves as they say the phrase):
- Χθες ήθελα να πάω, αλλά δεν μπορούσα.: Thumb over the shoulder ("yesterday"), reach out, then pull back.
- Είχα πολλή δουλειά.: Thumb over the shoulder, then mime a tall stack of papers.
- Δεν μπορούσα.: Thumb over the shoulder, then a head shake.
- Ήμουν στο σπίτι.: Thumb over the shoulder, then a roof over your head.

**Yesterday I wanted to….** Two friends catch up on yesterday with the anchor verbs in the past: είχα, ήθελα, μπορούσα, έβλεπα.

### Lesson 1: First day: what's in your bag?

Guess first (30 sec): "How do you think you'd say 'Hello (to a group, or polite)'?" Take two or three guesses, then teach Γεια σας.

Gestures (students make each one themselves as they say the phrase):
- Γεια σας: Big wave to the whole room, fingers together and slightly bent.

- Με λένε…: Hand flat on your own chest.
- Έχω ένα…: Hold the object up close to your body.

- Ναι, έχω.: Small Greek nod, head tipping down and to the side, and pat the object.
- Τι έχεις;: Both palms up, shoulders slightly raised.

**First day: what's in your bag?.** Two classmates meet before class and compare what they brought.

A: Γεια σου! — Hi!
B: Γεια σου! Πώς σε λένε; — Hi! What's your name?

A: Τι έχεις στην τσάντα; — What do you have in your bag?

A: Έχω ένα κινητό, ένα κλειδί και νερό. — I have a phone, a key, and water.
B: Έχεις ένα τετράδιο; — Do you have a notebook?
A: Όχι. Έχεις ένα στυλό; — No. Do you have a pen?
B: Ναι, έχω. Ορίστε! — Yes, I do. Here you go!
A: Ευχαριστώ! — Thank you!
B: Παρακαλώ. — You're welcome.
A: Γεια σου, Νίκο! — Bye, Nikos!
B: Γεια! — Bye!

### Lesson 2: Visiting the neighbor

Guess first (30 sec): "How do you think you'd say 'Would you like a little water?'?" Take two or three guesses, then teach Θέλεις λίγο νερό;.

Gestures (students make each one themselves as they say the phrase):

- …αλλά…: One hand out, then the other hand out on the other side.

Host: Γεια σου! Καλώς ήρθες! — Hi! Welcome!

Host: Θέλεις λίγο νερό; — Would you like a little water?

Host: Θέλεις τσάι; — Do you want tea?
Guest: Ναι, θέλω τσάι, ευχαριστώ. — Yes, I'd like tea, thank you.
Host: Έχω και ένα γλυκό. Θέλεις; — I also have a sweet. Would you like one?
Guest: Όχι, ευχαριστώ. — No, thank you.
Host: Λίγο; Θέλεις λίγο; — A little? Do you want a little?
Guest: Ναι, λίγο! Ευχαριστώ. — Yes, a little! Thank you.
Host: Νίκο, θέλεις κι εσύ τσάι; — Nikos, do you want tea too?
Nikos: Όχι, θέλω καφέ. — No, I want coffee.
Host: Ο Νίκος θέλει καφέ, αλλά δεν έχω καφέ! Έχω τσάι. — Nikos wants coffee, but I don't have coffee! I have tea.
Nikos: Ναι, τσάι! — Yes, tea!

### Lesson 3: Can you make it?

Guess first (30 sec): "How do you think you'd say 'May I?'?" Take two or three guesses, then teach Μπορώ;.

Gestures (students make each one themselves as they say the phrase):
- Μπορώ;: Reach toward an object and pause, eyebrows up.
- Ναι, βέβαια.: Big open-handed "go ahead" sweep.
- Μπορείς;: Point at the partner and tap your wrist.
- Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ.: Reach out eagerly, then pull back and shake your head.
- Μπορείς σήμερα το βράδυ;: Point down at the floor ("today"), then lay your head on your hands ("evening").
- Λυπάμαι, δεν μπορώ.: Hand on chest with a sad face, then a small head shake.
- Αύριο το πρωί μπορώ.: Roll a hand forward ("tomorrow"), then stretch both arms up as if waking.
- Έχεις ώρα;: Tap your wrist.

**Can you make it?.** Two friends ask permission and try to find a time to meet.

A: Γεια σου, Νίκο! Μπορώ; (points to the empty chair) — Hi, Nikos! May I?
B: Ναι, βέβαια! — Yes, of course!
A: Έχεις ώρα σήμερα; — Do you have time today?
B: Όχι σήμερα. Γιατί; — Not today. Why?
A: Θέλω καφέ. Μπορείς σήμερα το βράδυ; — I want a coffee. Can you make it tonight?
B: Λυπάμαι, δεν μπορώ. Θέλω, αλλά δεν μπορώ. — Sorry, I can't. I want to, but I can't.
A: Αύριο το πρωί; — Tomorrow morning?
B: Αύριο το πρωί… ναι, μπορώ! — Tomorrow morning… yes, I can!
A: Τέλεια! Και η Μαρία; Μπορεί; — Perfect! And Maria? Can she?
B: Η Μαρία δεν μπορεί το πρωί. Μπορεί το βράδυ. — Maria can't in the morning. She can in the evening.
A: Καλά. Αύριο το πρωί εμείς, και το βράδυ η Μαρία! — Okay. Tomorrow morning us, and in the evening Maria!
B: Εντάξει! Γεια! — Okay! Bye!

**Fork it**

- Person shift: ask about someone else (Έχει…; Θέλει…;).
- Tile round (can replace the slot-swap replay): pairs build three scene sentences from the Lesson 6 tiles in the textbook, on the sentence mat. Then flip to a question, add δεν, swap one tile, and write the last version by hand.
- Retell round (last replay): each student retells the scene to a new partner three times, with less time each time (60, 45, then 30 seconds). The listener only listens.
- Mix-in: work one review line into a replay, for example Δεν έχω…

A good pattern is three replays: the first straight, the second with one slot swap, and the third as the retell round.
