## 2 · Warm-up

> [!example]- Warm-up script
> **Word list (1 min):** students black-mark today's 12 new words on their printed list: αν (25) · η φορά (84) · το χέρι (158) · δίνω (161) · το κεφάλι (257) · το πόδι (275) · ο γιατρός (278) · ο λαιμός (680) · πονάω (723) · το δόντι (848) · η πλάτη (1425) · η κοιλιά (1426)

> **Guess first (30 sec):** "How do you think you'd say 'Christ is risen! – Truly He is risen!'?" Take two or three guesses, even wild ones, then teach **Χριστός Ανέστη! – Αληθώς Ανέστη!**. Wrong guesses still help it stick.  
>

> > Students make each gesture themselves every time they say the phrase. Watching you do it is not enough.
> >
> > | Phrase | Meaning | Gesture |
> > |---|---|---|
> > | **Χριστός Ανέστη! – Αληθώς Ανέστη!** | Christ is risen! – Truly He is risen! | Pass the flame: touch your candle to the partner's. |

> **Step 1: the Easter greeting**  
> T: Χριστός Ανέστη! — Ss: Αληθώς Ανέστη!

> T: (touches each) το κεφάλι, ο λαιμός, η κοιλιά, το δόντι, το χέρι, το πόδι, η πλάτη.  
> **Step 3: Με πονάει…**  
> T: (mimes a headache) Με πονάει το κεφάλι. — Ss repeat, then each mimes and says their own.  
> **Step 4: at the pharmacy counter**  
> T: Έχετε κάτι για το κεφάλι; · Μπορείτε να μου δώσετε κάτι για τον λαιμό; · Έχω πυρετό. · Δεν μπορώ να κοιμηθώ.

## 3 · Main scene

**Christ is risen! At the pharmacy**: The Easter greeting, then a visit to the pharmacy for a headache.

**Familiarity:** 96% of the words are known after the warm-up · **New in this scene:** αν, ο γιατρός

- A: **Χριστός Ανέστη!** — Christ is risen!
- Pharmacist: **Αληθώς Ανέστη! Τι θέλετε;** — Truly He is risen! What can I get you?
- A: **Με πονάει το κεφάλι. Έχετε κάτι για το κεφάλι;** — I have a headache. Do you have something for it?
- Pharmacist: **Ναι, βέβαια. Πονάει και ο λαιμός σας;** — Yes, of course. Does your throat hurt too?
- A: **Όχι, ο λαιμός μου είναι καλά. Αλλά δεν μπορώ να κοιμηθώ.** — No, my throat is fine. But I can't sleep.
- Pharmacist: **Έχετε πυρετό;** — Do you have a fever?
- A: **Δεν ξέρω.** — I don't know.
- Pharmacist: **Ορίστε. Ένα χάπι τρεις φορές την ημέρα. Αν δεν είστε καλύτερα, πηγαίνετε στον γιατρό.** — Here you go. One pill three times a day. If you don't feel better, go to the doctor.
- A: **Ευχαριστώ. Πόσο κάνει;** — Thank you. How much is it?
- Pharmacist: **Τέσσερα ευρώ. Περαστικά!** — Four euros. Feel better soon!

## 4 · Fork it

> [!tip]- Swaps, twists, and replays
> Papers face-down for every replay. Peek only when stuck, then put the paper back.
>
> **Swap one slot per replay**
>
> - What hurts: το κεφάλι · ο λαιμός · η κοιλιά · το χέρι · το πόδι
> - Form: Με πονάει το κεφάλι. · Πονάει ο λαιμός μου.
> - How often: μία φορά την ημέρα · δύο φορές · τρεις φορές
> - Mix-in: work one line from today's review into a replay, for example **Καλή Ανάσταση!**
>
> **Twists**
>
> - Symptom charades: mime a symptom, and the class says the Με πονάει… sentence.
> - The pharmacist keeps asking more questions. The patient has to answer each one.
>
> **Every lesson**
>
> - Role swap: A and B trade parts.
> - Person shift: retell it about someone else (έχει, έχουμε, έχουν).
> - Time shift: as yesterday (ήθελα, πήγα…) or as tomorrow (θα…).
> - Retell round (last replay): each student retells the scene in Greek to a new partner three times, with less time each time (2 minutes, 90 seconds, then 1 minute). The listener only listens.
> - Tile round (can replace the slot-swap replay): give the English, and pairs build the sentence from their tiles on the sentence mat. Then flip it to a question with ;, add δεν, swap one tile, and write the last version by hand. A: "My head hurts." · B: "Do you have a fever?" · C: "I can't sleep."
>
> Three replays: first straight, then with one slot swapped, then the retell round. Change only one thing per round.
>
> **Recall 2 (1 min):** papers face-down. Give the English for three warm-up phrases you didn't use in Recall 1. Students answer in Greek, with the gesture.

> [!note]- Exit ticket, homework, watch-list
> **Recall 3 · Exit ticket:** One Με πονάει… sentence, then the Easter greeting and its answer. Papers away. Students write it by hand on an index card, then say it. They keep the cards, and the red ones come back next week.
>
> **Homework (10 min a day)**
> - Point to 5 body parts at home and say Με πονάει… for each.
> - Greet three people with Χριστός Ανέστη! this week.
>
> **Out in the world:** Learn the Easter greeting by heart and use it with a Greek speaker if you meet one: Χριστός ανέστη! — Αληθώς ανέστη! Write a get-well card in Greek: Περαστικά!
>
> **Watch-list:** λαιμός is le-MOS (αι = e). Αληθώς is a-li-THOS. πονάει is po-NA-ee. Students say "έχω πόνο" for everything; push Με πονάει….
