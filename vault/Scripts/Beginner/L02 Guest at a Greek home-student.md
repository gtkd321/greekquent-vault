## Today's phrases

Make the gesture every time you say the phrase. It helps the phrase stick.

| Phrase | Meaning | Gesture |
|---|---|---|
| **Θέλεις λίγο νερό;** | Would you like a little water? | Offer an imaginary glass with both hands. |
| **Ναι, θέλω λίγο…, ευχαριστώ.** | Yes, I'd like a little…, thank you. | Pinch thumb and finger close together for "a little", then hand to heart. |
| **Όχι, ευχαριστώ.** | No, thank you. | Greek "no": tilt the head up with a small click of the tongue, then hand to heart. |
| **Θέλεις ένα γλυκό;** | Would you like a sweet? | Offer an imaginary spoon of spoon-sweet. |
| **Έχεις ψωμί;** | Do you have bread? | Mime tearing a piece of bread, eyebrows up. |
| **Ο Νίκος θέλει τσάι.** | Nikos wants tea. | Point at "Nikos", then mime holding a hot cup and blowing on it. |
| **…αλλά…** | …but… | One hand out, then the other hand out on the other side. |

## Main scene

**Visiting the neighbor**: A guest arrives at a Greek neighbor's house. In Greece a host offers more than once, and a first "no" is expected.

**New words:** καλώς ήρθες, ο καφές, δεν

- Host: **Γεια σου! Καλώς ήρθες!** — Hi! Welcome!
- Guest: **Γεια σου, Μαρία! Ευχαριστώ.** — Hi, Maria! Thank you.
- Host: **Θέλεις λίγο νερό;** — Would you like a little water?
- Guest: **Ναι, θέλω λίγο νερό, ευχαριστώ.** — Yes, I'd like a little water, thank you.
- Host: **Θέλεις τσάι;** — Do you want tea?
- Guest: **Ναι, θέλω τσάι, ευχαριστώ.** — Yes, I'd like tea, thank you.
- Host: **Έχω και ένα γλυκό. Θέλεις;** — I also have a sweet. Would you like one?
- Guest: **Όχι, ευχαριστώ.** — No, thank you.
- Host: **Λίγο; Θέλεις λίγο;** — A little? Do you want a little?
- Guest: **Ναι, λίγο! Ευχαριστώ.** — Yes, a little! Thank you.
- Host: **Νίκο, θέλεις κι εσύ τσάι;** — Nikos, do you want tea too?
- Nikos: **Όχι, θέλω καφέ.** — No, I want coffee.
- Host: **Ο Νίκος θέλει καφέ, αλλά δεν έχω καφέ! Έχω τσάι.** — Nikos wants coffee, but I don't have coffee! I have tea.
- Nikos: **Ναι, τσάι!** — Yes, tea!
- Guest (to the class): **Η Μαρία έχει ένα γλυκό. Ο Νίκος θέλει καφέ, αλλά…** — Maria has a sweet. Nikos wants coffee, but…

## Practice

> [!tip]- Replays, swaps, and twists
> Replay the scene with a partner, with the page face-down. Peek only when you get stuck. Change one thing each time.
>
> **Swap one thing**
>
> - Drinks: νερό · τσάι · καφέ · κρασί · γάλα
> - Food: ένα γλυκό · ψωμί · λίγο τυρί
> - Answer: Ναι, θέλω λίγο… · Όχι, ευχαριστώ. · Καλά, λίγο!
> - An old line: work in one review phrase, for example **Γεια σας**
>
> **Twists to try with a partner**
>
> - The host insists three times. The guest has to hold out until the third offer, then accept.
>
> **Every time**
>
> - Trade parts with your partner.
> - Ask about someone else (Έχει…; Θέλει…;).
> - Retell round: tell the whole scene in Greek to three different partners, with less time each time (60, 45, then 30 seconds). When you're listening, just listen.

> [!example]- Build it with tiles
> Cut out your Lesson 2 tiles (shaded tiles are verbs). Lay out the sentence mat, then for each sentence:
>
> 1. Build it from the English, then read it aloud.
> 2. Turn it into a question: swap the . for ; and raise your voice at the end.
> 3. Make it negative: put δεν right before the verb.
> 4. Swap one tile for another word from the extra tiles.
> 5. Write your last version by hand in your notebook.
>
> - **A:** Would you like a little water?
> - **B:** Yes, I'd like a little water, thank you.
> - **C:** Nikos, do you want tea too?
>
> Extra tiles: δεν · ; · . · νερό · τσάι · καφέ · κρασί
