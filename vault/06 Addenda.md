> [!question]- A2 · Fluent in a Year pace and word order (open: revisit after student testing)
> **Added:** 2026-11-13 · **Where:** [[07 Fluent in a Year]]
>
> The Fluent in a Year tier asks for 5 new words a day, 6 days a week. Most of those words come straight from the frequency list, nudged toward the week's scene, so some weeks hold abstract words (η κατάσταση, το σύστημα) that no lesson uses yet.
>
> **Fallback:** If students fall behind, keep 5 a day but add catch-up days to the two Christmas rest weeks, or cut the tier's goal to 1,000 words by May and finish the list over the summer. If abstract words don't stick, add a short scene sentence per week built from them. Change `fiay.py` and rebuild.
>
> **Watch for in class:**
> - How many days a week did students actually do their 5 words?
> - Which weeks' words felt random or useless?
> - Did the scene task and group meet prompt get them using the words, or only reading them?

> [!question]- A5 · The 85% rule: aim for about 15% misses (open: test in class)
> **Added:** 2026-11-13 · **Where:** [[03 Class Flow and Spaced Repetition]], [[07 Fluent in a Year]], [[09 Class Games and App Ideas]]
>
> Andrew Huberman cites a 2019 Nature Communications paper (Wilson, Shenhav, Straccia and Cohen, [The Eighty Five Percent Rule for optimal learning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6831579/)): learning is fastest when you get about 85% right and miss about 15%. Huberman stresses "about" ([Huberman Lab](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/the-science-of-setting-and-achieving-goals)). Caveat: the math was worked out for two-choice tasks in machine-learning and animal-learning models, not tested on people learning vocabulary.
>
> **Fallback:** Tune difficulty toward about 1 miss in 7. **Buzzer:** it is a two-choice task, the exact setup in the paper. The end screen now shows each player's hit rate; over a few games, above 95% means pick a later lesson or mix in more, below 70% means go earlier ([[09 Class Games and App Ideas]]). **Anki:** the default desired retention is 90% ([Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)); try 85% with a few volunteers (fewer reviews, more misses). **Sunday R/Y/G:** about 4 to 5 of the week's 30 words still red or yellow is on target; almost all green means add next week's words early, more than 8 red means slow down.
>
> **Watch for in class:**
> - Do buzzer hit rates land between about 75% and 90%, and does moving lessons fix it when they don't?
> - Do the Anki volunteers at 85% remember as much by Sunday, or just feel discouraged?
> - How many of the 30 words are red or yellow on Sunday?

> [!question]- A10 · Graph exploration: which version to keep (testing: calm native graph with Word web controls)
> **Added:** 2026-11-14 · **Where:** [[11 Release Notes]], [[09 Class Games and App Ideas]]
>
> The teacher likes how lively Obsidian's own graph feels, but not that it keeps drifting for seconds after you touch it. The Word web is calm but feels less alive. Options so far (details in [[11 Release Notes]]): (1) Obsidian's local graph as it is, with only the whole-vault graph turned off. It feels alive but keeps drifting after a touch. (2) The local graph with calmer settings: lower its four force sliders (center, repel, link strength, link distance) and save that as the default. This shortens the wobble, but Obsidian has no setting for how fast it settles and its graph has no public plugin API, so the drift can't be switched off completely. (3) Word web, first version: a fixed ring; words slide but lines jump. (4) Word web, second version (current): one smooth glide, pan, zoom and drag, no movement when idle. (5) Possible third version: a short burst of real physics, using the same kind of open-source force simulation Obsidian's graph is built on, tuned to settle within about a second, so it feels alive when touched and then goes still. It could have a Calm or Lively switch.
>
> **Now testing (2026-11-14):** option 2 is the default. The left toolbar button "Open word graph" opens Obsidian's local graph full-screen with calm forces: center 0.9, repel 3, link 1, distance 120 (Obsidian's defaults are 0.52, 10, 1 and 250). The sidebar graph uses the same settings. The Word web is still there as the command "Open word web (custom view)". To go livelier (option 1), open Forces in the graph and raise repel toward 10.
>
> **Update (Student Layout 1.4):** the settings box is hidden and the graph has the Word web's toolbar, trail and click-to-move. Notes open from the Open note button (touch and hold was tried and removed in 1.4.1).
>
> **Fallback:** Ship the vault with both the local graph (option 1 or 2) and the Word web (option 4). Watch which one students reach for, then decide whether to build option 5.
>
> **Watch for in class:**
> - Which one do students open without being told?
> - Does anyone feel queasy, and with which version?
> - Do they explore longer in the lively graph or the calm web?
> - Do they open the word notes from the graph, or just browse?

> [!question]- A11 · Look up a word: does it help or distract? (testing: Student Layout 1.7)
> **Added:** 2026-11-14b · **Where:** [[11 Release Notes]], [[04 How Memory Works]]
>
> Goal: let students reach for words they want (creative and free) without drifting away from the 1,500. The search checks the 1,500 first, so most look-ups lead back to a word note and its graph. Words outside the list open in Google Translate as bonus words and are logged in My Work.
>
> **Why it should help:** looking a word up because you need it for something you want to say adds "need" and "search", two of the three parts of the involvement load hypothesis, and higher involvement tends to mean better retention ([Studies in Second Language Acquisition](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/involvement-load-hypothesis-plus/5C5984B326F37FBF3A3DDA3C0EB2996C)). Guessing before seeing the answer (pretesting) improves later recall even when the guess is wrong ([Kornell and Vaughn](https://sites.williams.edu/nk2/files/2011/08/Kornell.Vaughn.inpress.pdf)); a meta-analysis found a moderate effect, g = 0.54 ([Psychonomic Bulletin and Review](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13423-023-02353-8.pdf)).
>
> **Caveat:** for beginners, looking words up isn't automatically better than being given them ([IJLLS](https://ijlls.org/index.php/ijlls/article/download/2757/1237)). The look-up only pays off if the word gets used again.
>
> **Watch for in class:**
> - Read a few students' "Words I looked up" notes. What share are from the 1,500 vs bonus words?
> - Do bonus words cluster on a theme? Those could become a scene or a band-2 word swap.
> - Does anyone use Guess first without being told?
> - Bring 2 or 3 looked-up words back as review the next week.

> [!question]- A12 · Ask the group in GroupMe (testing: Student Layout 1.7)
> **Added:** 2026-11-14b · **Where:** [[11 Release Notes]]
>
> GroupMe has no free way for an app to drop a message straight into a group. Its bot API posts as a bot and needs a secret token, which can't sit in a public vault. So the button builds the message and hands it to the phone's share menu, where the student picks GroupMe and the class group. The link points to the note on GitHub, which opens in any browser.
>
> **To add a "Join the class group" button:** in GroupMe, open the group, then Settings, then Share group, and copy the link ([GroupMe help](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/groupme/how-do-i-create-a-share-link-in-groupme)). Paste it into GROUPME_JOIN at the top of the Student Layout plugin's main.js and publish.
>
> **Watch for in class:**
> - Does GroupMe show up in the share menu on iPhone and Android?
> - Does the GitHub link preview cleanly in GroupMe?
> - Do questions get answered by classmates, or only by the teacher?
