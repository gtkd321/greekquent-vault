# 08 Online Course Studio Plan

Goal: use the audio studio and a black backdrop to film Greekquent lessons, so the course can be sold online as well as taught in person. Links: [[00 Greekquent Index]] · [[06 Addenda]] · [[07 Fluent in a Year]]

> [!summary]- Backdrop: what to buy
> - **Best:** duvetyne. It is a heavy, matte cotton that soaks up light, so it looks truly black on camera. Muslin is thinner, lets more light through, and wrinkles more ([Expo WHS](https://expowhs.com/blogs/news/best-fabric-for-stage-backdrops)).
> - **Budget:** black muslin works if it is lit well. Adorama sells Liba duvetyne by the roll and a Glow 6x9 ft black muslin for about $21.95 ([Adorama](https://www.adorama.com/lists/draping-fabrics)). B&H carries a 10x12 ft Impact black muslin and a wrinkle-resistant Westcott X-Drop ([B&H](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/buy/muslin-fabric-backgrounds/ci/23716/N/3659994756)).
> - **A black bedsheet** can work for a first test if it is pulled tight and has no creases ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2014/03/a-cheap-easy-way-to-create-a-professional-looking-infinite-black-backdrop)).
> - Size: at least 6 ft wide for a seated, waist-up shot. Go wider (9 to 10 ft) if you want to stand or gesture.

> [!summary]- Lighting: keep the black black
> - Sit about 5 ft in front of the backdrop so light from your face doesn't land on it ([Joe Edelman](https://www.joeedelman.com/portrait-lighting-arrangements-for-diy-led-studio-lights)).
> - **Key light:** one soft light close to you, about 45 degrees to one side. Light drops off fast with distance, so a close key lights you and leaves the backdrop dark. Avoid umbrellas because they throw light everywhere, including onto the background ([YouTube walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mdqr-FrEbug)).
> - **Rim light:** a small light behind you and to the side, at 30 to 45 degrees, so your outline separates from the black. Keep the background 2 to 3 stops darker than the rim ([rim light guide](https://flashboards.yaroflasher.com/learn/lighting/rim-light/)).
> - Wear a mid-tone or light top, not black, so you don't disappear into the backdrop.
> - Sound: use the studio mic you already have. A quiet, treated room matters more than the camera.

> [!summary]- What to film for each lesson
> - **Intro and mission (1 to 2 min):** the scene for the week and what students will be able to do.
> - **Phrases with gestures (3 to 5 min):** each chunk said slowly, then at natural speed, with the gesture from the book.
> - **Scene performance (1 to 2 min):** the full scene, start to finish, as a model.
> - **Letters and pronunciation (Lessons 1 to 6):** the four letters of the week, close up, with sounds.
> - **Fork round demo:** how students swap words in and out of the scene.
> - **Fluent in a Year clips (30 to 60 s each):** the 5 words of the day in a phrase. That is 300 short videos over the year, and they double as free samples and marketing.

> [!summary]- Rules
> - Do not use the Teach Yourself audio in your videos. That audio is licensed for students who own the book, so point students to it instead of playing it on camera.
> - Film in batches: one lesson per session, all sections, the same outfit and light.
> - Keep each video short (under 8 minutes). Shorter clips are easier to redo and easier for students to replay.

> [!summary]- Pilot plan
> 1. Hang the backdrop, set up two lights, and film a 2-minute test. Check that the background reads as pure black and your edges are clean.
> 2. Film all of Lesson 1.
> 3. Show it to current students and ask: is it clear, is it the right length, and would they pay for it? Record the answers in [[06 Addenda]] (item A4).
> 4. Then decide where to host it: Skool or another free/low-cost community platform, matching the tools already used for the class.
